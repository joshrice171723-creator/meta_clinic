# Business Model

## Confirmed business boundary

The formed consultancy sells a done-for-you launch package with ongoing support, including a starter SharePoint system. The EHR/inventory entity is not yet formed and will have different ownership. Its intended pricing is an implementation fee plus recurring charges calculated as a percentage of ordered inventory value. Track revenue, delivery cost, support cost, and IP separately; no intercompany fee or bundling agreement is supplied. See [[Entity and Product Boundary]].

The founder expressed no deadline pressure. This does not supply a cash budget, unlimited labor, or an approved spending level.

- Consultancy launch package: fee amount, payment milestones, and outside-cost coverage explicitly TBD.
- Ongoing consultancy support: intended offer confirmed; scope, duration, and charging arrangement explicitly TBD.
- Software: implementation fee plus a percentage of ordered inventory value, charged recurringly. Rate, eligible orders/value, payer, billing trigger/interval, minimums/caps, and bundling remain open.
- Video knowledge bank with mentorship: additional service concept; selling entity, packaging, and pricing unassigned. See [[Knowledge Bank and Mentorship]].

The chosen pricing basis is ordered inventory value, not encounters or patient-care revenue. Document the actual commercial and procurement relationships for review under L-04 in [[Texas Diligence]]; do not infer legality or an undisclosed vendor commission from the pricing description.

## Inventory-value pricing — basis confirmed, mechanics open

Conceptual variable fee = agreed percentage × eligible value of inventory ordered during the billing period. This describes the founder's selected basis, not a completed billing specification or price quote.

Before implementing billing, define eligible inventory and orders; which party pays the fee; how orders/value are captured; placement versus payment/fulfillment as the trigger; discounts, tax, shipping, cancellation, partial fulfillment, returns, credits, currency, and duplicate-order treatment; billing period; reporting/disputes; and any minimum/cap. Clarify whether only orders placed through the system are included and whether any distinct EHR charge exists. No percentage or order-volume forecast is assumed.

## Done-for-you cost boundary — definition needed

Executing a task does not tell us who funds it. Clarify whether package pricing includes or excludes attorney and filing fees, premises/buildout, licenses, insurance, equipment, supplies, and vendor subscriptions. Identify approval and payment authority for purchases or submissions; the provider's initial intake is not assumed to authorize unlimited spending or signature on their behalf.

## Simple economics to maintain

- Project contribution = project revenue − delivery labor − subcontractor costs − project-specific vendor/payment costs.
- Monthly subscription contribution per clinic = monthly revenue − variable infrastructure/vendor costs − attributable support labor.
- Inventory-linked recurring contribution = collected inventory-value fees − attributable order-processing/vendor/payment/support costs. Evaluate EHR operating costs too if this component is intended to fund the combined product.
- Acquisition cost per new customer = attributable sales/marketing expense and labor ÷ new paying customers in the measured cohort.
- Cash runway = available business cash ÷ net monthly cash burn, only when burn is positive and assumptions are explicit.

These are planning definitions, not forecasts. Track cash payment timing separately from revenue and margin. Separate our company's economics from the MD clinic's startup budget and patient-care revenue.

## Commercial decision gates — proposed

Do not scale a service until delivery hours and costs are visible. Assess recurring EHR/inventory economics within the software entity and ongoing support within the consultancy; neither should assume the other's revenue or uncompensated labor. Include Kevin's physician/mentorship time and any backup arrangements in the appropriate cost model. Identify intercompany charges explicitly. Do not enter unknown inputs as zero.

A pricing experiment should state the exact scope, proposed price, buyer, response, objections, commitment obtained, and delivery-cost estimate. “Interested” is weaker evidence than a scoped paid engagement.
