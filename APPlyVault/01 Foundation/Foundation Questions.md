## Round 2 — once a first segment is clear

- What is the smallest deliverable someone would pay for, and what is explicitly excluded?
- Who signs the engagement and controls the budget? What other people influence the purchase?
- What spending and launch-delay evidence exists? What alternatives do customers use today?
- Will the company ever access patient information, including during configuration or support?
- Which qualified advisors will review entity structure, service boundaries, privacy/security, and customer-facing claims?
- Who owns the MD's templates, procedures, and implementation? What rights may this company receive?
- Does the company already exist legally? What founder ownership, decision rights, exit terms, and IP assignments remain to be resolved?
- Is physician matching, delegation coordination, credentialing, billing, or clinic management in scope?
- What recurring problem remains after launch? Could the honest business be primarily consulting at first?
- Who owns customer acquisition, delivery, product, research maintenance, and weekly vault review?

## Round 3 — before a pilot offer or build commitment

- What scope, price, payment terms, prerequisites, and cancellation terms will we test?
- What must be reviewed before we can deliver each part safely and credibly?
- What baseline, outcome, labor, and rework metrics will we measure?
- What evidence would cause us to stop, narrow the market, change the offer, or avoid building software?
- Which dates and owners are actual commitments rather than aspirations?
- What support, incident response, access, portability, and offboarding obligations would the software create?
