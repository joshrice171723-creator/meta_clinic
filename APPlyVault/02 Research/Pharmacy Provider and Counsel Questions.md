# Pharmacy Provider and Counsel Questions

Home: [[00 Home]] · Legal issues: [[Texas Diligence]] · Packet: [[Clinic Launch Packet]] · Product: [[Pharmacy Inventory Data Requirements]] · Sources: [[Source Register]] · Follow-up: [[Roadmap and Tasks]]

## Purpose and use

Prepare a practical discussion with the proposed clinic's NP/PA providers, delegating physician, a Texas pharmacist familiar with the intended setting, and Texas healthcare/pharmacy counsel. This responds to the founder's request to record the internal-pharmacy findings and a comprehensive set of open questions. It is preliminary issue spotting, not legal advice, a completed professional review, or permission to operate.

**All 60 questions below are open for the actual clinic.** Provider descriptions establish facts and preferences; counsel and responsible professionals determine applicability and sufficiency. A proposed reviewer is not an engaged advisor or an accepted commitment. Use only relevant sections for a given scenario and document the reason for any non-applicability.

Preserve D-028: shared software design supports clinics choosing and combining setups. No single pharmacy model must be selected before synthetic foundation work. Before a particular clinic uses a capability, however, its operator, site, activity, products, professional authority and controls need their own evidence. A software setting, pharmacy application or source link does not grant that authority.

The consultancy, separate software company, Kevin's physician services/reference clinic, customer clinic, and any pharmacy operator remain distinct. The selected APPly software fee basis is a percentage of ordered inventory value; its rate and mechanics remain open. This discussion does not reopen that direction or assume the fee is legally cleared.

Describe the intended workflow without patient examples.

## A. Intended activities and clinic facts

**Proposed discussion lead:** clinic providers and operator. **Issues:** L-01/L-02/L-06/L-07.

- **PQ-01 — Activities:** For each proposed location, are drugs only prescribed to an outside pharmacy, administered on site, supplied for immediate needs, provided as free samples, dispensed for home use, compounded/repackaged, or transferred? Which activities will be combined?
- **PQ-02 — Formulary:** Which exact products, strengths, forms, routes and package sizes are contemplated? Identify Rx/OTC, controlled, refrigerated/frozen, hazardous, compounded and public-program products. What is expressly excluded initially?
- **PQ-03 — People and setting:** Is this an NP-led, PA-led, physician-led or other arrangement, and what is the actual facility type? Who owns and operates the clinical practice and any pharmacy, and at which physical sites?
- **PQ-04 — Recipients and handoff:** Only established clinic patients, or also outside prescriptions? Adults/minors? Pickup, caregiver collection, home visits, mobile services, mail/courier or interstate delivery? What quantities and repeat supplies are intended?
- **PQ-05 — Practical purpose:** Is the aim immediate access, convenience, lower patient cost, treatment administration or an additional revenue stream? What volumes, operating hours, staffing and storage capacity can the providers substantiate? Unknowns stay unknown.

## B. Legal pathway and pharmacy classification

**Proposed discussion lead:** Texas healthcare/pharmacy counsel with pharmacist input. **Issues:** L-01/L-02/L-03/L-06.

- **PQ-06 — Activity-by-activity authority:** For each activity in PQ-01, is a pharmacy license required, or what precise practitioner/facility authority applies? Identify the statute, current rule, conditions and limits; which proposed activities cannot proceed under that pathway?
- **PQ-07 — License class:** If licensed dispensing is planned, is Class A/A-S appropriate? Does Class D's physician-office exclusion or another eligibility restriction affect this exact NP/PA structure? Is there a qualifying institutional setting for any Class C proposal?
- **PQ-08 — Practitioner supply:** Under Chapters 157/158 and current board rules, what qualifies as immediate needs, whose patients may be served, what quantity/duration is permitted, and what may an NP, PA or other trained staff member provide? What separates this from retail dispensing?
- **PQ-09 — Claimed exceptions:** If anyone proposes a samples, rural, charitable, public-health or other exception, what facts prove every condition? For §158.003, verify geography/population, pharmacy availability and board notifications; neither Sherman/North Texas nor “small clinic” establishes eligibility.
- **PQ-10 — Alternative arrangements:** Could an independently licensed pharmacy supply patient-labeled medications, co-locate, deliver, or operate an approved remote service? Who holds the license and stock, and what restrictions apply to clinic storage/handoff? Obtain current requirements for the exact arrangement; no remote-services shortcut is assumed.

## C. Provider authority, delegation and staffing roles

**Proposed discussion lead:** NP/PA, delegating physician and pharmacist as applicable, with counsel. **Issues:** L-02/L-06; T-15.

- **PQ-11 — Credentials and scope:** Which active Texas licenses, APRN prescriptive authority, PA/physician arrangements and registrations are required for each person and location? Are there population, specialty, drug-category or disciplinary restrictions relevant to the proposed services?
- **PQ-12 — Written arrangements:** What prescriptive authority agreement, standing orders or protocols are needed? Do they address the actual drugs, locations, consultation, emergencies, quality review and alternate physician? What registration, review, meeting and documentation duties apply?
- **PQ-13 — Action permissions:** Who may order stock, receive it, access storage, prepare/repackage, label, clinically check, administer, provide, dispense, counsel and document waste? Which acts require a pharmacist, and which may be delegated under this specific pathway?
- **PQ-14 — Coverage:** When must the physician or pharmacist be present, available or supervising? What happens during breaks, after hours, leave, license lapse or loss of the delegating physician/PIC? Which affected activities stop while patient care and stock custody continue safely?
- **PQ-15 — Competency and accountability:** Who verifies training, maintains role/credential evidence, reviews errors and updates protocols? How are Kevin's clinical oversight and backup distinguished from mentorship, consultancy support and software support?

## D. Licensed pharmacy, premises and application work

**Applies if the proposed pathway requires a pharmacy or remote-service approval. Proposed lead:** operator, prospective PIC and counsel. **Issues:** L-01/L-03/L-06/L-08.

- **PQ-16 — Ownership/application:** Who is the direct pharmacy owner, who are the managing officers, and what formation, ownership, tax-status, disclosure and background information is required? Is that structure compatible with the clinic, professional control and related-company arrangements?
- **PQ-17 — Pharmacist coverage:** Who will be PIC and supply pharmacist/technician coverage? What employment, hours, supervision, registration and backup requirements apply? Have lawful operating hours and the cost of coverage been distinguished from clinic hours?
- **PQ-18 — Physical space:** What separate secured area, access controls, alarm, refrigeration, equipment, counseling space and facilities are required? Do the lease/use, floor plan and local zoning, occupancy, fire and accessibility approvals permit the intended operation?
- **PQ-19 — Application sequence:** Which current packet, fees, supplier letter, attestations/notarization and inspections are needed? Who prepares, verifies, signs, pays and submits? At what point may equipment and drug stock be obtained, and what evidence permits opening?
- **PQ-20 — Changes and renewal:** How do ownership, managing officers, location, PIC, hours or service changes affect a pending or issued license? Which notices, new applications, inspections and renewal/recheck dates must be tracked? Avoid commitments based on an unapproved application.

TSBP's public form contains sensitive identity-document and SSN requirements. Collect any required real application information only through approved controlled storage/submission, never this vault, chat or synthetic portal. Its service checkboxes do not independently authorize a class of practice. [Class A packet, version 09/25; S-63](https://www.pharmacy.texas.gov/files_pdf/licensing/LIC-Class_A.pdf). Personnel/security questions also draw on selected TSBP §§291.32–291.34, with current-rule reconciliation still required. [S-25](https://www.pharmacy.texas.gov/files_pdf/TSBP%20Rules_MASTER%20FILE.pdf).

## E. Patient charges, samples and payment arrangements

**Proposed discussion lead:** clinic operator and counsel; CPA/payer expertise where needed. **Issues:** L-04/L-06/L-07.

- **PQ-21 — Complete charge map:** What does the patient pay for the visit, membership, drug, dispensing, supplies, administration and delivery, including bundled or indirect charges? Under the chosen legal pathway, which charges are permitted and how must they be described?
- **PQ-22 — Free samples:** Who receives and controls samples, and under whose professional authority? How will the applicable original-packaging, no-direct-or-indirect-charge, labeling and record conditions be met? Could any membership allocation or charge undermine the proposed sample pathway?
- **PQ-23 — Purchased take-home stock:** If purchased medication will leave with patients, what specifically authorizes the supply and each proposed charge? If “at cost” is proposed, what legal basis and cost definition apply? Do not infer rural eligibility or general dispensing authority from the price.
- **PQ-24 — Payer/program exposure:** Are there Medicare/Medicaid beneficiaries, opt-out/private-contract issues, Part D or other insurance claims, public vaccine stock or assistance programs? What payment, enrollment, patient-notice and program restrictions apply despite the clinic's cash-pay positioning?
- **PQ-25 — Patient choice and disclosures:** May patients use an outside pharmacy and receive the necessary prescription? What ownership/financial-interest disclosures, pricing notices, receipts, substitution consent and refund/complaint processes are required for the actual arrangement?

## F. APPly, procurement, ownership and financial relationships

**Proposed discussion lead:** the relevant entity owners and counsel. **Issues:** L-01/L-04/L-08; T-09/T-14.

- **PQ-26 — Goods and money:** At order, receipt, storage, use and return, who is buyer, seller, owner, custodian and payer? Will either APPly entity buy, take title, broker, warehouse, distribute or resell drugs, or only supply software/administrative services?
- **PQ-27 — Distribution and transfers:** If APPly or a clinic arranges shared purchasing, central storage, consignment, drop shipping, borrowing or transfers between clinics, what state/federal licensing and transaction rules apply? What changes when ownership or the legal operator changes?
- **PQ-28 — Selected software fee:** Given the agreed percentage-of-ordered-inventory-value basis, how should counsel evaluate the actual services, payer, fee calculation, eligible orders and financial relationships? What contractual safeguards or revisions, if any, are needed before billing? No rate or legal conclusion is assumed.
- **PQ-29 — Incentives and referrals:** Are supplier rebates, discounts, commissions, free equipment/software, preferred-pharmacy referrals, shared ownership, leases or physician-service payments contemplated? Which Texas solicitation/fee/control rules and federal AKS/Stark provisions or exceptions apply, and what disclosures/evidence are needed?
- **PQ-30 — Contract and decision rights:** Which entity signs supplier, pharmacy, software and professional-service contracts? Who approves purchases and substitutions, carries loss/return risk and receives credits? How is independent clinical/pharmacist judgment preserved, including the right to reject a purchase or referral?

## G. Inventory, storage, tracing and returns

**Proposed discussion lead:** pharmacist or responsible medication clinician, procurement and operations. **Issues:** L-03/L-06. Collection detail: [[Pharmacy Inventory Data Requirements]].

- **PQ-31 — Authorized sourcing:** How will the actual supplier, product and transaction eligibility be verified? What evidence is needed at receipt for product/package, quantity/unit, lot, expiry, condition, invoice and discrepancies? Who can release a questionable shipment?
- **PQ-32 — Physical controls:** Who can access each cabinet/refrigerator, and how are purchased, sample, consigned, program, patient-specific and held stock separated? What counts, reconciliation, discrepancy escalation and opening/closing checks apply?
- **PQ-33 — Cold chain and containers:** What manufacturer/program instructions determine storage, monitoring, calibration, opening/reconstitution dating and use-by times? Who handles missing readings, power failures and excursions, and who authorizes release or disposal of affected stock?
- **PQ-34 — DSCSA and recalls:** Which entity/products/transactions are covered, which exceptions or dated exemptions apply, and what eligibility evidence is needed? Who maintains tracing records, investigates suspect/illegitimate product, makes required notifications and reconciles stock with protected patient follow-up?
- **PQ-35 — Returns and disposal:** How are supplier returns, patient returns, administration waste, expired stock and recalled/controlled/hazardous products handled differently? Which recipients, disposal contracts, manifests, credits and reporting are required? What prevents unsupported restocking or an unexplained negative adjustment?

Existing S-25/S-26 and S-30–S-38 provide bounded background on counts, tracing, storage, PMP and disposal. The FDA small-dispenser extension is date- and eligibility-specific, not a blanket waiver. Recheck current controlling text and actual applicability before defining operating procedures; this guide does not set universal count, retention or disposal rules.

## H. Controlled substances

**Conditional on the actual products/activities. Proposed lead:** responsible registrant, clinicians/pharmacist and counsel. **Issues:** L-02/L-06/L-07.

- **PQ-36 — Registration:** Which DEA registration category, registrant, authorized schedules and site coverage are needed for prescribing, purchasing, storing, administering and dispensing? Do staff exceptions apply, and where is separate registration required? Do not assume one physician's number covers every person/entity/site.
- **PQ-37 — NP/PA limitations:** What schedule, quantity, refill, age, consultation and setting limitations apply to delegated prescribing? Distinguish prescribing from administration under a physician order, and validate any claimed Schedule II facility/hospice exception.
- **PQ-38 — Acquisition and custody:** What ordering authority, forms/electronic ordering, secure storage, access, initial/periodic/event-triggered inventories and acquisition/disposition records apply? How will observed counts reconcile with the ledger and any required separate records?
- **PQ-39 — PMP and prescribing systems:** Who must check PMP, when, and with what exceptions/documentation? Who must report dispensing, with what deadline, acknowledgments and correction process? Does electronic prescribing require separately reviewed identity/signature functionality? An inventory export is not a completed regulatory submission.
- **PQ-40 — Diversion, loss and disposal:** Who investigates discrepancies, protects evidence and determines theft/significant loss? Which agencies/forms/deadlines apply? How do destruction, wastage, reverse distribution and patient take-back differ, including witnesses and registration requirements?

The DEA manual is official 2023 guidance, not a substitute for current regulations or a site-specific registration finding; direct current eCFR reconciliation remains open. TSBP's PMP page states next-business-day reporting for Texas pharmacy dispensing of Schedules II–V; the exact workflow and exceptions must be reviewed. [S-26](https://www.deadiversion.usdoj.gov/GDP/%28DEA-DC-071%29%28EO-DEA226%29_Practitioner%27s_Manual_%28final%29.pdf), [S-37](https://www.pharmacy.texas.gov/PMP/).

## I. Compounding, repackaging and special products

**Conditional scope; aesthetics is not added to the launch offer. Proposed lead:** pharmacist/clinical specialist and counsel. **Issues:** L-02/L-03/L-06.

- **PQ-41 — Preparation classification:** Does any activity involve mixing, diluting, reconstituting, splitting packages, drawing up syringes, relabeling or preparing batches? Which is administration preparation, repackaging or compounding under the applicable rules? Distinguish finished sterile products from sterile compounding.
- **PQ-42 — Source pathway:** Will compounded drugs come from a 503A pharmacy, a registered 503B outsourcing facility or an on-site practitioner/pharmacy? Is patient-specific prescribing required, or is the contemplated office stock lawful? What state licenses and supplier/product checks remain necessary?
- **PQ-43 — Facility and quality:** Which current state rules and incorporated standards, including relevant USP chapters, apply to sterile/nonsterile preparation or hazardous handling? What facilities, equipment, training, environmental/quality testing and pharmacist coverage are needed?
- **PQ-44 — Preparation evidence:** Who approves formulas/instructions, input lots/quantities, batch/container genealogy, labeling, beyond-use dating and release? How will sterility/quality concerns, waste, recall and outsourced preparation records be handled?
- **PQ-45 — Product/program restrictions:** Do vaccines, hazardous drugs, REMS products, biologics, investigational products or any other proposed category create additional conditions? If weight-management, hormones or aesthetic products are later requested, what separate source, preparation, prescribing and marketing review is needed?

This is a specialist question set, not a complete compounding or USP specification. FDA's laws overview was reviewed; the full product-specific rules, standards and supplier evidence were not. [S-67](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/human-drug-compounding-laws).

## J. Records, privacy and software boundaries

**Proposed discussion lead:** clinic/pharmacy record custodians, privacy/security lead, engineers and counsel. **Issues:** L-05/L-06; T-09/T-13.

- **PQ-46 — Required records:** What prescription/order, clinical review, administration/supply, fill/partial-fill/refill, label, counseling, handoff and correction evidence is required for each pathway? Which system is authoritative, and who signs/checks each event?
- **PQ-47 — Custody and access:** Which entity is responsible for each record, and which staff may see it? How will inventory operators, consultancy staff and software billing remain separated from patient-linked medication records, including opaque identifiers and audit trails?
- **PQ-48 — Applicable privacy duties:** How do HIPAA, Texas medical-privacy law and any special record restrictions apply to the actual clinic, pharmacy and vendors? Which exact services/data paths need agreements and controls, including SharePoint, hosting, logs, support, backups, delivery and reporting?
- **PQ-49 — Retention and rights:** What distinct retention, access/amendment, export, legal-hold and disposal rules apply to clinical, prescription, controlled-drug, tracing, purchasing and audit records? How will records remain accessible if a supplier portal, software contract or clinic closes?
- **PQ-50 — Safe operation and validation:** What workflow, access, audit, reconciliation, downtime, backup/restore and incident tests must pass before real use? How will versioned configuration and permissions block unsupported actions without losing required history or recall access?

L-05 remains open. Only wholly synthetic examples belong in the vault and prototypes; no patient records or patient-derived examples are requested. These questions do not approve the current Rails slice, a SharePoint workflow or any cloud service for PHI.

## K. Patient safety, incidents, insurance and continuity

**Proposed discussion lead:** responsible clinicians/pharmacist, operator, insurer and counsel. **Issues:** L-02/L-05/L-06/L-07/L-08.

- **PQ-51 — Clinical checks:** Who reviews the prescription/order, allergies, interactions, appropriateness and substitutions; performs the final check; supplies required counseling/instructions; and documents a refusal, error or correction? What equipment and emergency response are needed for on-site administration?
- **PQ-52 — Event response:** How will medication errors, adverse reactions, recalls, suspected counterfeit product, diversion and privacy incidents be escalated? Who protects evidence, determines reportability/deadlines and makes patient, board, agency, vendor or insurer notifications?
- **PQ-53 — Insurance:** Do the actual policies cover the intended dispensing, administration, compounding, professional oversight, delivery, product liability, cyber events and stock/cold-chain losses? What exclusions, limits, tail/continuity terms and contractual allocations need review?
- **PQ-54 — Downtime and absence:** What happens if the EHR, dispensing system, refrigerator, power, supplier or required professional is unavailable? Which activities pause, how are patients routed safely, and how is later record/stock reconciliation controlled?
- **PQ-55 — Closure or transition:** If a clinic, pharmacy or vendor closes, changes owners or terminates service, who safeguards and lawfully transfers/returns/disposes of stock, preserves records, supports patient continuity, gives required notices and addresses licenses/registrations?

**Proposed discussion lead:** clinic operator and applicable professional reviewers; consultancy coordinates only its agreed work. **Issues:** L-01–L-08; T-05/T-12/T-36.

- **PQ-57 — Included work and authority:** Which pharmacy deliverables, external fees, staffing, buildout, insurance and continuing obligations belong in the consultancy engagement? - **PQ-58 — Readiness evidence:** What written scope findings, licenses/registrations, facility approvals, agreements, policies, training and operational rehearsals are prerequisites to each real activity? Who has authority to accept each item and make the scoped opening decision?

## Record answers and evidence
