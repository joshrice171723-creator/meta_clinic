# Customer Discovery

## Objective

Turn reported local demand into specific prospects and delivery requirements for the selected offer: helping Texas APPs open cash-pay primary-care / concierge-adjacent clinics. Learn purchasing readiness, scope, pricing, and recurring software needs. Keep customer evidence distinct from the MD reference clinic and broader market observations.

Current target: both NPs and PAs in Sherman/North Texas, purchasing a done-for-you launch package with ongoing support. Founder is unsure how many first applicants are available. Treat the pipeline count as unknown, not as zero or as validated demand for a specific package price.

## Demand already reported

On 2026-08-30, the founder described substantial local demand and increasing popularity of cash-pay PLLCs in primary care and adjacent areas such as Botox clinics. This is founder-reported market experience, not a quantified market-size or growth finding. The next useful step is to record concrete people, requested help, launch stage, and buying intent—not to make the founder prove that clinic-startup activity exists.

No lead names, specific conversations, launch budgets, or paid commitments have been supplied yet. Do not infer zero demand from that missing documentation. Keep aesthetics prospects separately tagged until accepted into scope.

## First research batch — proposed, not scheduled

Start with the existing local network and identify approximately three to five concrete prospective clients. A five-interview batch remains a proposed learning tool, not a prerequisite imposed on all work. Include people actively opening and people who recently opened; include someone who delayed or abandoned a launch if reachable. This is not a statistically representative market study.

Recruit through existing relationships first. No outreach has been sent by this work and no bookings are recorded in the vault. Record contact details only with appropriate permission; use participant IDs for research summaries.

## Interview guide

1. Tell me about the last concrete step you took toward opening your clinic.
2. What happened next? Where did progress slow down, and why?
3. How did you decide what applied to you? Who did you rely on?
4. What have you spent in money and time? What mistakes or rework occurred?
5. What solutions did you try or consider? Why did they work or fail?
6. Who decides whether to buy help, and what budget or timing constraints exist?
7. What would a useful completed outcome look like? How would you know it worked?
8. What problems do you expect to remain after opening?

Only then present a specific offer and ask about scope, price, objections, and a concrete next step. Avoid asking only whether the idea sounds good. Do not ask for patient examples or records.

## Proposed interpretation rules

- If at least three of the first five independently describe the same material problem, investigate a narrowly scoped offer. This is a prioritization signal, not proof of market fit.
- Test that offer with at least two qualified prospects; record actual commitments separately from interest.
- A first scoped paid pilot is stronger evidence than survey enthusiasm, but still not proof of repeatability.
- If the pain, buyer, or solution differs across interviews, narrow or change the segment before building.
- If the recurring need is weak, revise the subscription hypothesis rather than manufacturing one.

Record contrary evidence and rejected offers. Set actual pass / revise / stop criteria in [[Decisions]] before using them to commit significant spending.
