## Purpose and limits

Track questions, applicability, evidence, and professional review. This is **not** an exhaustive legal checklist or an approval to form, own, or open a clinic. No customer-specific structure or local permit pathway has been cleared. Obtain qualified Texas healthcare counsel for case-specific legal conclusions.

The first dependency is the actual clinic profile: profession/license, proposed owners and roles, services, location, delivery model, and payer model. Do not treat APPs as one interchangeable legal category or assume the MD's path transfers unchanged.

## Sourced starting points, checked 2026-08-30

- Texas SOS describes professional-entity ownership and governance restrictions and directs readers to private counsel for entity selection. This does not establish the permissible arrangement for a particular clinic. [Texas SOS formation FAQs](https://www.sos.texas.gov/corp/formationfaqs.shtml).
- TMB describes physician delegation and prescriptive-authority arrangements for APRNs and PAs. An entity filing alone does not answer the separate practice/delegation questions in our plan. [TMB prescribing and supervision](https://www.tmb.texas.gov/apply-renew/physician/prescribing-and-supervision).
- TMB's PA licensing page describes physician supervision in addition to licensure. Keep PA-specific validation separate from NP/APRN validation. [TMB physician assistant licensing](https://www.tmb.texas.gov/index.php/apply-renew/physician-assistant/physician-assistant-license-application).
- Texas patient-privacy guidance addresses both HIPAA and the Texas Medical Records Privacy Act. Include state privacy review rather than treating HIPAA as the whole review. [Texas Attorney General patient privacy](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/consumer-protection/health-care/patient-privacy).

The implications for our planning are inferences, not legal opinions. Guidance pages are starting references; current controlling law, effective dates, and applicability still require review.

## Closing an issue

Record the precise question answered, clinic profile, controlling sources/effective dates, written finding, named reviewer and review date, limitations, affected playbook steps, and recheck trigger. “Not applicable” requires a rationale. A source link or completed task is not itself legal clearance.

Recheck before client use and whenever law, guidance, locality, ownership, service mix, or delivery model changes. Archive superseded conclusions rather than silently overwriting their history.
